Instructor notes / the model behind the map

All brands and segments are fictional. X and Y are perceived positions, not actual prices or measured research. A brand's attractiveness to a segment is exp(−distance² / (2 × 24²)). Each segment also has a fixed no-purchase attractiveness of 0.65. Divide each attractiveness by the total to get choice shares, then weight segment shares by population. This is an illustrative distance model, not a calibrated market forecast.

The empty corner button moves Novo to a location with little competition and little nearby demand. A white space is a hypothesis, not proof of an opportunity. There are no costs or margins in this draft; market share alone is not profit.

Extension ideas: add segment-specific willingness to pay, let students import research coordinates, or charge for repositioning.
