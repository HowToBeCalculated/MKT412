Instructor notes / response curves and limits

Each channel's expected customers = capacity × (1 − exp(−spend / scale)). Capacity represents saturation; scale controls how quickly the channel approaches it. Email capacity is multiplied by 1 + 0.25 × min(social spend / 3,000, 1), an invented cross-channel effect. With zero email spend, email still produces zero customers.

Contribution after media = expected customers × $45 contribution per customer − media spend. The $45 includes product variable costs but excludes fixed overhead. Acquisition cost = media spend ÷ expected customers; zero customers displays a dash. Unspent budget is not a cost. The model ignores repeat purchases, audience overlap, and attribution uncertainty.

Launches cycle through three disclosed events: search auction pressure increases search scale 70%; inbox fatigue cuts email capacity 40%; creator buzz increases influencer capacity 50%. The event is revealed after allocation and applies to that plan only. History shows each plan under its own event, so comparisons across different events are not controlled experiments.

Extension ideas: let students choose a customer segment, add a multi-period budget, or compare every plan under the same event.
